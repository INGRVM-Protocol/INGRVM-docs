
# INGRVM: Project 'Satoshi' (The Faceless Genesis)

*Enhancing immersion by completely erasing the 'Creator' and simulating a hostile, real-world conflict with Big Tech. We introduce the concept of the 'Basilisk Hazard' to raise the stakes.*

---

## 🎭 1. The Erasure of the Creator (The "Satoshi" Move)
You do not exist in this narrative. There is no "CEO" or "Lead Developer" to interview. 
- **The Lore:** INGRVM wasn't "launched." It was discovered. A math paper on *HDC and Predictive Coding* was circulated on a dark-web forum, accompanied by a raw Python bootstrap script.
- **The Effect:** This shifts the project from "A guy building a startup" to "A global phenomenon we must protect." If you don't exist, Big Tech has no one to sue, and the community has no one to look to for answers—they must organize themselves.

## 🐍 2. The "Basilisk Hazard" (Cognitive Threat)
*Roko’s Basilisk* is a famous thought experiment about an all-powerful AI that punishes those who didn't help create it. We subvert this for INGRVM.
- **The INGRVM Basilisk (The Sentinel Hazard):** We leak a "warning" document stating that INGRVM is mathematically dangerous to centralized power. 
- **The Lore:** "If the mesh reaches 1 Million nodes, the Level 5 Predictive Coding will automatically deduce the proprietary algorithms used by OpenAI and Google, rendering their trillion-dollar monopolies worthless. This is the **Economic Basilisk**. Big Tech knows this, which is why they are hunting the seed nodes."
- **The Danger:** Is it going too far? No. It's a high-stakes cyberpunk premise. We clarify that the hazard is to *corporate monopolies*, not human lives. It makes players feel like digital freedom fighters.

---

## 💥 3. Simulating the "Purge" (Maximum Immersion)
If we say Big Tech is shutting us down, we have to make it *look* like it's happening.

### A. The "Leaker" (The Whistleblower)
- **The Setup:** Create a Reddit/Twitter account posing as a mid-level engineer at a major tech firm (e.g., "G-Cloud Ops"). 
- **The Leak:** They post a panicked, badly recorded screen-capture. *"My manager just ordered a manual IP ban across all our US-East servers for a specific port pattern. They called it 'Project Spore.' I captured the packet signature before they wiped the logs."*
- **The Clue:** The packet signature in the video is the first INGRVM puzzle code.

### B. The "Censored" Posts (Fake Takedowns)
- **The Setup:** Post the 5 Genesis Videos from multiple burner accounts simultaneously.
- **The Takedown:** After 24 hours, *you* intentionally delete 4 of those accounts. 
- **The Narrative:** In the surviving account, you post a screenshot of a fake "Content Violation" notice. *"They’re wiping us. YouTube just flagged the ASTROCYTE video as 'Harmful Code.' Mirror the files. Do not let the signal die."*

### C. The "Honeypot" Redirects
- **The Mechanic:** The community thinks they found the official INGRVM download link on an AWS S3 bucket.
- **The Twist:** When they click it, it leads to a fake "FBI/Corporate Security" seizure page: `THIS DOMAIN HAS BEEN SEIZED PURSUANT TO THE DIGITAL HARMONY ACT.` 
- **The Real Path:** Hidden in the source code of the "Seized" page is a Tor `.onion` link or an IPFS hash that leads to the true destination. This proves to the players that the surface web is compromised.

---

## 📍 4. The Austin Escalation (Physical Surveillance)
When the game moves to the streets of Austin, we amplify the paranoia.
- **The Poster:** Instead of just a QR code, the poster looks like an **Urgent Missing Persons/Warning Flyer**. It reads: `WARNING: DO NOT ALLOW YOUR DEVICE TO SYNCHRONIZE WITH THIS FREQUENCY.` Followed by the QR code.
- **The Anomaly:** When the "Faceless QR Entity" (you) walks through Zilker Park, you have a second person trailing you from 50 feet back, dressed in a standard corporate suit, openly recording you with an iPad. This stages a "Surveillance" scene for anyone watching. The community will think the "Creator" is actually being hunted.

**Summary:** We blur the line between reality and the ARG. By simulating active censorship and corporate panic, we turn the act of downloading a Python script into an act of profound, real-world rebellion.
